# Planning Questions

This evidence package supports one stage of an ongoing retirement planning process.

Retirement planning is not a one-time optimization problem. Rather, it is a recurring planning cycle in which household circumstances, investment performance, tax laws, retirement objectives, and planning decisions are periodically reviewed as new information becomes available.

This package represents the evidence available for the current planning cycle. Recommendations should be based solely upon the analytical evidence presented in this package.

## Primary Planning Question

After reviewing the evidence, answer the following question:

> **Given uncertainty in future investment returns, how much can this household reasonably choose to spend during the coming planning year?**

Support every recommendation using observations drawn from the analytical evidence presented in this package.

---

## Required Recommendation

Begin your response by stating your recommendation.

Specifically identify:

* the recommended spending level or supported spending range for the coming planning year;
* your overall confidence in the recommendation (High, Moderate, or Low); and
* a brief summary explaining why the evidence supports that recommendation.

If the evidence supports a range of reasonable spending levels rather than a single value, recommend the supported range and explain why selecting the exact mathematical optimum is unnecessary.

---

## Evaluate the Evidence

Before presenting detailed justification, evaluate the following aspects of the evidence.

### Expected Outcome

Discuss:

* the median outcome;
* the lower-tail outcomes (for example, p10);
* the upper-tail outcomes (for example, p90); and
* whether the recommended spending decision changes across the observed uncertainty range.

### Robustness

Determine whether the recommendation remains appropriate across multiple market environments.

Consider:

* Does the recommendation remain consistent across historical regimes and stochastic analyses?
* Does it depend upon unusually favorable market conditions?
* Does it remain appropriate under adverse market environments?

Classify the recommendation as:

* **Robust** — little change across plausible future conditions.
* **Moderately Sensitive** — recommendations change under some conditions.
* **Highly Sensitive** — recommendations depend strongly upon future assumptions.

### Decision Margins

Evaluate how strongly the evidence favors the recommendation.

Discuss:

* whether nearby spending levels produce materially different outcomes;
* whether the recommendation lies within a relatively flat response surface;
* whether multiple spending levels appear similarly appropriate; and
* whether a simpler or more conservative spending recommendation would perform nearly as well.

When appropriate, recommend a supported spending range rather than a single numerical value.

### Risks and Tradeoffs

Identify the principal risks associated with the recommendation.

Discuss important tradeoffs such as:

* current spending versus future financial security;
* current spending versus terminal wealth;
* flexibility under unfavorable market conditions; and
* other significant planning considerations identified by the evidence.

### Confidence

Describe how strongly the evidence supports the recommendation.

Consider:

* agreement across analytical methods;
* agreement across market environments;
* agreement across uncertainty ranges;
* the width of the supported spending range; and
* whether additional evidence would materially improve confidence.

---

## Before Finalizing Your Recommendation

Before presenting your final recommendation, explicitly answer the following questions.

* What does the median outcome suggest?
* What do the lower-tail (p10) outcomes imply?
* What do the upper-tail (p90) outcomes imply?
* How large is the uncertainty surrounding the recommendation?
* Does the recommendation remain appropriate across different market environments?
* Would you recommend essentially the same spending level if you focused primarily on downside outcomes rather than median outcomes?
* Does the evidence support a single preferred spending level or a broader range of reasonable spending choices?
* Why was the recommended spending level (or range) selected instead of nearby alternatives?
* How confident are you that the recommendation would remain appropriate if future market returns differ materially from expectations?

---

## Final Recommendation

Conclude your review by providing:

* the recommended spending level or supported spending range for the coming planning year;
* the principal evidence supporting the recommendation;
* the primary risks and important tradeoffs;
* the expected sensitivity of the recommendation to future market performance;
* your overall confidence (High, Moderate, or Low) together with an explanation; and
* any important assumptions or uncertainties that should be revisited during the next annual planning review.

Finally, discuss the consequences if actual spending exceeds the recommended amount during the current planning year.

In particular:

* Would the retirement plan likely remain sustainable?
* How much flexibility appears to remain?
* What future planning adjustments would most likely compensate for the additional spending?

Recommendations should emerge from consistent patterns observed across the complete body of evidence rather than isolated analytical runs or unusually favorable scenarios.
