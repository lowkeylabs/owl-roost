# Study Subsystem

The study subsystem provides the analytical guidance layer of ROOST.

Its purpose is to help users move from:

```text
What question am I trying to answer?
```

to:

```text
What evidence should be generated?
```

without requiring users to manually construct sessions, runs, sweeps, or low-level override configurations.

The study subsystem sits between:

```text
retirement questions
```

and

```text
execution workflows
```

and is responsible for transforming analytical intent into reproducible evidence-generation strategies.

---

# Conceptual Role

The core ROOST execution hierarchy is:

```text
Case
    ↓
Session
    ↓
Run
    ↓
Trial
```

This hierarchy answers:

```text
How was a result produced?
```

The study subsystem addresses a different question:

```text
What should be investigated,
and what evidence should
be generated?
```

The study subsystem therefore owns:

* Question discovery
* Question organization
* Question applicability
* Evidence generation guidance
* Analytical guidance

while remaining independent of execution realization.

---

# Layers

ROOST intentionally separates three layers.

## Definition Layer

The definition layer describes work that may be performed.

Conceptually:

```text
Study
    ↓
Question
    ↓
Scenario Family
    ↓
Experiment
```

The definition layer answers:

```text
What should be investigated?
```

The study subsystem owns the definition layer.

---

## Realization Layer

The realization layer contains evidence generated by executing analytical work.

Conceptually:

```text
Case
    ↓
Session
    ↓
Run
    ↓
Trial
    ↓
Evidence
```

The realization layer answers:

```text
What was executed?

What evidence was generated?
```

The workspace and execution subsystems own the realization layer.

---

## Interpretation Layer

The interpretation layer consumes evidence and produces guidance.

Conceptually:

```text
Evidence
    ↓
Interpretation
    ↓
Recommendation
    ↓
Decision
```

Interpretation may be performed by:

* Users
* Advisors
* Expert systems
* LLMs
* Future ROOST guidance engines

The study subsystem generates evidence.

It does not make decisions.

Decisions emerge from interpretation.

---

# Evidence-Centric Architecture

ROOST is intentionally question-driven.

Users rarely begin with:

```text
What sweep should I run?
```

Instead they begin with:

```text
Can I retire?

Should I retire?

When should I retire?

When should I claim Social Security?

Should I perform Roth conversions?

How much can I spend?
```

ROOST does not directly answer these questions.

Instead ROOST generates evidence capable of supporting those questions.

Conceptually:

```text
Question
    ↓
Evidence
    ↓
Interpretation
    ↓
Decision
```

The study subsystem therefore focuses on evidence generation rather than direct recommendation.

---


# Studies

A study organizes related retirement questions.

Examples:

```text
Retirement Readiness

Social Security Strategy

Tax Strategy

Market Uncertainty

Spending Sustainability
```

A study answers:

```text
What collection of related
questions should be explored?
```

Studies provide organizational structure and help users discover adjacent questions.

Questions may participate in multiple studies.

Studies are organizational entities.

Studies are not execution artifacts.

---

# Questions

Questions represent the primary user-facing analytical entities within ROOST.

Examples:

```text
Can I retire?

Should I retire?

When should I retire?

When should I claim Social Security?

Should I perform Roth conversions?

How much can I spend?
```

Questions define:

```text
What is the user trying
to understand?
```

Questions may:

* Participate in multiple studies
* Reference multiple scenario families
* Require assumptions
* Require additional data
* Require new methodologies
* Require future research

Questions consume evidence.

They do not directly generate evidence.

---

# Question Relationships

Questions form a relationship graph.

Questions may:

```text
Refine other questions

Suggest related questions

Depend upon prerequisite questions

Participate in multiple studies
```

Examples:

```text
Can I retire?
    ↓

How much can I spend?

When can I retire?

What are my major risks?
```

and:

```text
Should I claim Social Security?
    ↓

How much does claiming age matter?

How sensitive is the decision?

What happens if I delay claiming?
```

These relationships support:

* Discovery
* Recommendation
* Educational workflows
* Study navigation

# Scenario Families

A scenario family defines a reusable family of related what-if investigations.

Examples:

```text
Retirement Timing

Social Security Claiming

Roth Conversion Strategy

Spending Level

Market Regime

Longevity

Asset Allocation

Housing Decisions
```

A scenario family answers:

```text
What evidence should
be generated?
```

Examples:

```text
What happens if I retire
this year?

What happens if I retire
next year?

What happens if I retire
in five years?

What happens if I claim
Social Security now?

What happens if I claim
next year?

What happens if I convert
$50,000 to Roth?

What happens if I skip
conversions this year?
```

Scenario families organize evidence generation.

They do not provide recommendations.

A scenario family may support many questions.

A question may depend upon many scenario families.

Scenario families are expected to become the primary evidence-generation abstraction within ROOST.

---

# Experiments

A Experiment defines a methodology for generating evidence within a scenario family.

Examples:

```text
yearly_sweep

monthly_sweep

historical_windows

bootstrap_regimes

amount_sweeps

owl_optimizer
```

A Experiment answers:

```text
How should this scenario
family be explored?
```

Experiments may define:

* Required levers
* Override patterns
* Experimental methodologies
* Recommended starting points

Examples:

```text
roost_sweeps.ss_age_pair=
    62,63,64,65,66,67,68,69,70
```

Experiments belong to exactly one
scenario family.

Scenario-family-to-choice-template
relationships are derived from choice
template registrations rather than
maintained separately.

They are not execution artifacts.

---

# Levers

A lever represents a case-dependent capability test.

Examples:

```text
has_social_security

has_pretax_savings

has_retirement_timing
```

A lever answers:

```text
Can this investigation
be performed?
```

Levers may apply to:

* Questions
* Scenario Families
* Experiments

Conceptually:

```text
Case
    ↓
Lever Evaluation
    ↓
Applicable Experiments
    ↓
Applicable Scenario Families
    ↓
Applicable Questions
```

---

# Guidance and Remediation

Levers are expected to evolve beyond simple applicability checks.

A false lever should eventually help answer:

```text
Why can't this question
be answered?

What information is missing?

What assumptions are required?

How can the user proceed?
```

Potential guidance may include:

* HFP worksheet recommendations
* Data collection suggestions
* Assumption clarification
* Additional studies
* Alternative investigations

The goal is to transform:

```text
Question unavailable
```

into:

```text
Question unavailable today.

Here are the steps needed
to make it answerable.
```

# Annual Reevaluation

ROOST treats retirement planning as a sequence of annual reviews rather than a one-time optimization problem.

At the beginning of each year a household may reconsider:

```text
Retirement Timing

Social Security Claiming

Roth Conversions

Spending Levels

Asset Allocation

Housing Decisions
```

Many of these investigations naturally produce:

```text
Do it now

Do it next year

Delay

Defer

Revisit later
```

alternatives.

ROOST is designed to help users understand the consequences of each alternative before committing to action.

---

# Decision Flexibility and Sensitivity

ROOST is not solely concerned with finding optimal outcomes.

ROOST also seeks to understand:

```text
Decision Sensitivity

Decision Flexibility

Decision Urgency

Decision Optionality

Safe Deferral
```

A flat response surface may indicate:

```text
Low Sensitivity

High Flexibility

Low Urgency

Safe Deferral
```

A steep response surface may indicate:

```text
High Sensitivity

High Urgency

Additional Investigation Required
```

Understanding whether a decision operates within a forgiving range of outcomes may be more valuable than identifying a mathematically optimal choice.

For many retirees, reassurance that multiple reasonable choices produce similar outcomes is more valuable than finding a single optimal answer.

# Question Answerability

Questions exist on an answerability spectrum.

Examples:

```text
Answerable Today

Requires Assumptions

Requires Additional Data

Requires New Methodology

Requires Research
```

Questions are often answered indirectly through evidence.

The study subsystem may recommend scenario families capable of generating the evidence required to address a question.

Future versions of ROOST may use answerability analysis to identify:

* Missing HFP data
* Missing methodologies
* Missing scenario families
* New research opportunities

# Registry Architecture

The study subsystem follows the same registry-driven architecture used throughout ROOST.

Registration ownership belongs to individual modules.

Conceptually:

```text
StudySpec
    registered by studies/

QuestionSpec
    registered by questions/

ScenarioFamilySpec
    registered by scenario_families/

ChoiceTemplateSpec
    registered by experiments/

LeverSpec
    registered by levers/
```

Relationships flow downward:

```text
Study
    ↓
Question
    ↓
Scenario Family
    ↓
Experiment
    ↓
Lever
```

This allows:

* Question reuse
* Multi-study participation
* Independent evolution
* Reusable evidence-generation strategies

# Architectural Invariants

Studies organize questions.

Questions are first-class user-facing entities.

Scenario families define evidence spaces.

Experiments belong to scenario
families and define methodologies.

Scenario-family relationships are
derived from registered choice
templates.

Levers determine applicability.

Experiments generate evidence.

Evidence supports interpretation.

Interpretation supports recommendations.

Recommendations may influence decisions.

The study subsystem does not own decisions.

The study subsystem defines work that should be performed.

The realization subsystem generates evidence.

The interpretation layer consumes evidence.

Execution artifacts remain outside the study subsystem.

The study subsystem is expected to become the primary analytical guidance layer within ROOST.

Future capabilities may include:

* Case-aware question recommendations
* Scenario-family recommendations
* Automatic lever detection
* Evidence generation
* Session generation
* HFP guidance workflows
* Educational walkthroughs
* Sensitivity analysis
* Flexibility analysis
* Safe-deferral analysis
* Research-gap discovery
* Reproducible research workflows

The long-term goal is to help users:

```text
Discover Questions

Understand Requirements

Generate Evidence

Understand Sensitivity

Understand Flexibility

Identify Deferrable Decisions

Interpret Results

Make Decisions
```

while preserving the existing execution hierarchy and provenance model.
