# Evidence Overview

This evidence package contains the results of multiple retirement planning analyses performed under different assumptions regarding future investment returns. The purpose of these analyses is to evaluate how a proposed retirement strategy performs across a variety of plausible market environments.

Because future investment returns cannot be predicted with certainty, the evidence is intended to support planning decisions by illustrating the robustness and sensitivity of the proposed strategy under changing market conditions.

The evidence is organized into two complementary parts.

## Experimental Design

The first section describes the analytical runs included in this study.

Each analytical run evaluates the same household, retirement objectives, planning assumptions, and decision variables. The runs differ primarily in the methodology used to model future investment returns. Depending on the experiment, market returns may be represented using historical replay, stochastic bootstrap sampling, deterministic return assumptions, or other analytical methods.

Understanding how the analytical runs differ provides the context necessary to interpret the results that follow.

## Results

The remaining tables summarize the outcomes produced by each analytical run.

For stochastic analyses, three statistical summaries are presented.

* **Median** — the typical outcome observed across all simulated trials.
* **10th Percentile (p10)** — a conservative outcome representing the lower end of the simulated distribution.
* **90th Percentile (p90)** — a favorable outcome representing the upper end of the simulated distribution.

Deterministic analyses produce a single outcome. Consequently, the reported values are identical for the median, p10, and p90 summaries.

Each results table reports a common set of retirement planning metrics for every analytical run. Depending on the planning objective, these metrics may include first-year spending, lifetime spending, taxes, Roth conversions, terminal wealth, or other measures relevant to retirement planning.

Reporting a consistent set of metrics across every analytical run enables direct comparison of retirement outcomes under different market environments.

## Purpose of the Evidence

The evidence presented in this package is intended to support comparative analysis rather than prediction.

No individual analytical run should be interpreted as a forecast of future market performance. Instead, the collection of analytical runs illustrates how the proposed retirement strategy responds to a range of plausible investment return environments.

The objective of this study is not to determine which market environment is most likely to occur. Rather, it is to evaluate whether the proposed retirement strategy remains appropriate despite uncertainty regarding future investment returns.

Subsequent sections describe the analytical methods, present the resulting evidence, and provide guidance for interpreting the results when evaluating retirement planning decisions.
