# Reading the Results

The following tables summarize the outcomes produced by the analytical runs described earlier in this evidence package.

Each row represents a complete evaluation of the same household, retirement objectives, and planning assumptions performed under a different investment return methodology or market environment. Unless otherwise indicated, the retirement strategy itself remains unchanged between analytical runs. Differences in the reported outcomes therefore reflect differences in investment return assumptions rather than changes to the retirement plan.

The tables are intended to support comparison across multiple lines of evidence rather than evaluation of any single analytical run in isolation.

## A Structured Review Process

Review the evidence in a consistent order.

For each planning decision:

1. Examine the statistical summaries (p10, median, and p90) within each analytical run to understand the uncertainty associated with that market environment.
2. Compare the results across multiple analytical runs to determine how outcomes change under different investment return assumptions.
3. Look for patterns that remain consistent across both uncertainty ranges and market environments.
4. Base planning recommendations on the overall body of evidence rather than any individual numerical result.

This progression emphasizes robust planning decisions supported by multiple independent lines of evidence.

## Comparing Results

The primary purpose of these tables is comparison.

Rather than focusing on a single row, compare outcomes across multiple analytical runs.

Consider questions such as:

* Which retirement outcomes remain relatively stable across different market environments?
* Which planning metrics are most sensitive to changes in investment returns?
* Which market environments place the greatest stress on the retirement strategy?
* Do nearby planning decisions produce materially similar outcomes?
* Are the observed differences large enough to influence a planning decision?

A retirement strategy that performs reasonably well across many market environments is generally more robust than one that performs exceptionally well under only a narrow range of assumptions.

## Understanding the Statistical Summaries

Stochastic analyses report three statistical summaries.

* **Median** — the typical outcome observed across all simulated trials.
* **10th Percentile (p10)** — a conservative outcome representing the lower end of the simulated distribution.
* **90th Percentile (p90)** — a favorable outcome representing the upper end of the simulated distribution.

Together, these values describe the range of plausible outcomes produced by a given analytical run.

A narrow range between p10 and p90 suggests relatively consistent outcomes across simulated trials.

A wider range indicates greater sensitivity to investment return uncertainty.

Deterministic analyses produce a single outcome and therefore report identical values for the median, p10, and p90 summaries.

## Evaluating Robustness

No individual analytical run should be interpreted as a prediction of future market performance.

Instead, the collection of analytical runs should be viewed as complementary evidence describing how the proposed retirement strategy responds to a variety of plausible future investment environments.

When reviewing the results, consider whether the proposed retirement strategy:

* maintains acceptable spending across many market environments,
* avoids unacceptable downside outcomes during unfavorable markets,
* produces reasonably consistent tax outcomes,
* preserves planning flexibility as market conditions change, and
* remains appropriate despite uncertainty in future investment returns.

Greater confidence exists when similar planning conclusions are supported by multiple analytical methods despite differing assumptions.

## Drawing Conclusions

The evidence presented in this study should be used to identify strengths, weaknesses, sensitivities, and tradeoffs within the proposed retirement strategy.

Avoid conclusions that depend upon a single market environment, a single statistical summary, or a small numerical advantage.

Instead, base conclusions on patterns that remain consistent across multiple analytical methods and statistical summaries.

When several nearby planning decisions produce materially similar outcomes, treat them as a supported planning range rather than searching for a single mathematical optimum. Preference may reasonably be given to recommendations that are simpler, more conservative, easier to explain, or easier to revisit during future planning cycles.

Retirement planning decisions that remain effective across a broad range of plausible market environments are generally more robust than decisions whose success depends upon one particular view of future investment returns.
